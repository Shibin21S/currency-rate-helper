Frequently Asked Questions

Plain-language answers about how this project works

How does this project work?

Every day, an automated script fetches the real, live Bank of Canada baseline exchange rate for five currencies (USD, GBP, EUR, JPY, INR) and uses it to estimate what each of the five major Canadian banks would likely charge. It then highlights which bank offers the best estimated deal for each currency.

How are the estimated bank rates calculated?

Each bank's estimated rate is the live Bank of Canada baseline multiplied by that bank's own publicly documented typical markup percentage. For example, if a bank's typical markup is 2.75%, its estimated buy rate is the baseline times 1.0275, and its estimated sell rate is the baseline times 0.9725.

Why are bank-specific rates estimated instead of live?

Bank websites display their exchange rates using JavaScript calculators that compute the number directly in your browser. That means the actual rate never exists as plain text anywhere in the page — there's nothing for an automated fetch to read, confirmed directly across all five banks' sites. Rather than show nothing, this project uses each bank's own published markup to produce a transparent, clearly-labeled estimate instead.

How often does the data update?

Automatically, once a day, via a scheduled GitHub Actions workflow. The Bank of Canada baseline shown is always real, live data from that day's (or the most recent business day's) fetch.

Why does TD often appear as the best deal?

In this model, the "best deal" is simply the bank with the lowest published markup percentage — and TD's published markup is currently the lowest of the five banks compared. That makes TD win across most currencies by design, not because of anything specific to any single currency. If a bank's published markup changes, or a bank with a lower markup is added, the results will change accordingly.
